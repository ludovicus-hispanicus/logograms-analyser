---
genre: diagnostic omens
period: Middle Babylonian
provenance: Susa (Elam)
recension: Elamite periphery
counting: line
topic: prognosis; Hand-of-DN diagnoses (Adad, Kubu, Šamaš, steppe/open-country, Šulak, qajjalu, rabitu, mašda-of-the-roof); one fatal verdict
series: pre-canonical medico-magical tablet (No. XI = Suse XII/9-10); the diagnostic omen-entries only, ritual/prayer columns excluded
canonical_parallel: Schmidtchen 2021 concordance (mB Susa column, book p.711) cites MDP 57 Nr. 11 ii 1 = Sakikkû 13:63(?) and ii 2 = Sakikkû 14:71 — both uncertain, and Susa is the SOLE witness for them (no cross-witness parallel partner in the corpus). Individual entries otherwise echo TDP Hand-of-DN diagnoses. NB an earlier note here read 11:68-69; that was a column-misalignment of the concordance and is corrected.
edition: Labat & Edzard 1974, MDP 57, No. XI (Suse XII/9-10), transcription pp. 241-247; hand-copies Figs. 26-29
dating: the Susa XII literary group; Schmidtchen 2021 files it as mB (Middle Babylonian) Susa. An Old Babylonian horizon is possible for the Susa group (cf. the Ḫattuša early-import problem). Counted as Middle Babylonian per Schmidtchen.
note: >
  ONLY the medical/diagnostic omens are scored here. The tablet is medico-
  magical: cols. III, IV, VI are dominated by long syllabic RITUAL and PRAYER
  instructions (sacrifices, fumigations, the Ea-šarru prayer) which are NOT
  diagnostic-series text and are OMITTED, to keep this comparable with the
  other Sakikkû witnesses. Included: every šumma-marṣu (...) qāt-DN entry from
  cols. II, III, IV, V, VI. Each entry is given on one line (tablet line refs
  in [brackets after] each); counting:line is only for segmentation, the LDI
  is a token ratio independent of it.
  Orthography after Labat's transcription (logograms uppercase, normalized
  reading was in parentheses in the edition). The opening šumma-sign, which
  Labat copies as U₄, is rendered UD here (= the same sign u₄) so it is treated
  as the formulaic opening particle, exactly like DIŠ in the canonical series.
  Diagnoses are partly logographic (ŠU {d}IŠKUR, ŠU {d}UTU, ŠU {d}ku-bi) but
  several deity-names are spelled SYLLABICALLY (ŠU šu-la-ak = qāt Šulak,
  ŠU qa-a-a-li = qāt qajjāli, ŠU ra-bi-ti = qāt rabīti) — the peripheral trait.
  Logograms uppercase, syllabic lowercase, {} determinative, [] restoration,
  x illegible, < > scribal omission supplied by the editor.
